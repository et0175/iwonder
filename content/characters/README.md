# The cast

One file per character. Copy `_template.md`, name it after them in kebab-case
(`nina.md`), and they appear on the site the next time it is built.

The field that matters is **`role`** — what the character is *for*. Personality
is easy and can be changed later; function cannot. If two characters have the
same role, one of them is decoration.

## Roles this kind of book tends to need

Not a prescription — a checklist to hold your own cast against. It is fine to
cover several of these with one character, and fine to leave some empty.

| Role | Why it earns its place |
|---|---|
| **The asker** | Says the question out loud in a child's words. Without them the article has no beginning. |
| **The one who is wrong on purpose** | Gives the intuitive wrong answer — the Sun goes behind the hill, the seasons are about distance. A child holding that belief needs to see it held by someone likeable, not corrected by a narrator. |
| **The one who does it** | Reaches for the ball and the torch. Turns talk into an experiment. |
| **The pedant** | Asks the annoying follow-up that shows the first answer was too easy. This is what stops the book being smug. |
| **The one who doesn't care** | Wants to know why it matters. Keeps the book honest about relevance. |
| **The grown-up who doesn't know** | Adults in children's books are usually oracles. One who says *I don't know, let's find out* is worth more than any of them. |

## A caution

Characters who exist to explain things turn the book into a lecture with names
on it. The concepts in `content/concepts/` are designed so that a *phenomenon*
does the explaining. Characters are there to be curious, to be wrong, and to
do the experiment — not to know the answer in advance.
