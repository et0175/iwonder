## What they can see

Spinning on an office chair; the playground roundabout; turning your head slowly with one eye shut.

## What they ask

> Why does the room run away when I spin?

## Short answer

When you rotate, everything stationary appears to sweep past in the opposite direction. The apparent motion of the surroundings and your own rotation are the same event described from two viewpoints, which is the whole of the day-and-night argument in miniature.

## The experiment

Spin on the spot and **name what goes past**: door, window, dog, door again — all
of it the other way round. Then spin the other way: the same things, in reverse
order. The room cannot be turning both ways, and it always turns against whoever
is spinning.

The control is whoever did not spin. For them nothing moved at all. (A dog sitting
in the middle of the room does this job better than a person, because nobody
suspects a dog of being polite about it.)

## Watch out

**Two different questions live in one spin, and only the first one belongs here.**

- *While* you turn, the room sweeps past the other way — that is this concept, and
  it is the day-and-night argument in miniature: your turning and the world's
  sweeping are one event told from two seats.
- *After* you stop, the room keeps going for a few seconds — that is **not** this
  concept and must not be answered with it. The mechanism is in the ear: the
  semicircular canals are loops filled with fluid, lined with hair cells; the
  spin sets the fluid moving and it bends them, and when the body stops the fluid
  keeps going by inertia for a while, so the signal says «ще крутимось».
