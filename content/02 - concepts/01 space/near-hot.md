## What they can see

Hands held towards a campfire; stepping back from the oven door.

## What they ask

> Why is it hotter when I stand close to the fire?

## Short answer

Heat and light spread out from a source in all directions, so the amount reaching you drops steeply with distance: at twice the distance you receive a quarter. This single relationship later explains both the seasons and why the outer planets are frozen.

## The experiment

Walk towards the oven (or a campfire, or a radiator) in even steps, hand out, and say out loud at every step whether it is warmer than the last one. The prediction a child makes without noticing is that even steps give even warmth. They do not: three steps of almost nothing, then suddenly too hot to stay.

Then the ladder, with the hand and counted paces: at twice the distance the warmth is not half but about a quarter. Same hand, same fire, same air.

## Watch out

**The fact does not surprise anyone — the unevenness does.** "Closer is warmer" is something a five-year-old already knows, so a story built on the fact has nothing to give. The story is built on the broken prediction: *I walked evenly and the warmth did not grow evenly.* That sentence is the whole concept, and a child can produce it herself.

Two more things this concept has when surprise runs out:

- A wrong answer worth having. "The oven gave all its heat to the cake, there was none left for the room" is generous, sensible and false — and it is the kind a reader could have said. The scene holds on that, not on the fact.
- Scale, saved for later. The same unevenness makes winter and summer, and keeps the far planets frozen. Near the oven it is a kitchen fact; at the Sun it is why there is a January. Do not spend that payoff in the oven scene — it belongs to the story that needs it.
