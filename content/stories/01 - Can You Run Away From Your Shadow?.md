# Can You Run Away From Your Shadow?

Max was running across the garden.

He stopped.

His shadow stopped.

He jumped.

His shadow jumped.

He ran backwards.

His shadow ran backwards too.

“This is annoying,” said Boy.

Kira looked up. “What is?”

“My shadow. It follows me everywhere.”

Strange looked down at Max’s shadow.

“Have you asked it to stop?”

“No.”

“Good idea,” said Strange. He bent down. “Excuse me! Could you stay here for a minute?”

The shadow didn't answer.

“Maybe it doesn't understand our language,” said Strange.

Max had a better idea.

“I'll run away from it.”

He raced across the garden.

Strange raced after him.

Kira followed more slowly.

When Max reached the fence, he turned around.

The shadow was still there.

“It's very fast,” said Strange.

“Let's trick it,” said Max.

They ran around the tree.

The shadow followed.

They jumped over a flower bed.

The shadow followed.

Max suddenly ducked behind the garden shed.

His shadow disappeared.

Strange stopped.

“You did it!”

Max carefully looked around the corner.

His shadow appeared again.

He pulled his head back.

Gone.

Out.

There.

In.

Gone.

Kira came around the shed.

“That's strange.”

“Thank you,” said Strange.

“Not you. The shadow.”

They tried again.

Max stood in the sunshine.

Shadow.

Max stepped behind the shed.

No shadow.

Sunshine.

Shadow.

Behind the shed.

No shadow.

Professor Ada was sitting nearby, writing something in her question notebook.

Kira looked at the ground.

“Professor Ada, where does a shadow go when it disappears?”

Professor looked toward the Sun.

Then at Max.

Then at the shed.

“Perhaps,” she said, “the better question is: what makes it appear?”

Strange inspected the ground.

“The ground?”

Max moved his hand in front of his face.

For a moment the sunlight disappeared from his eyes.

He moved his hand away.

Light.

Hand back.

Dark.

Kira picked up a small piece of cardboard.

She held it in the sunlight.

A dark shape appeared on the ground.

She moved the cardboard.

The dark shape moved.

She tilted it.

The shape changed.

Then she held the cardboard behind the shed.

Nothing.

“The Sun can't get here,” she said.

Boy looked from the Sun to the cardboard and then to the dark patch on the ground.

“So the shadow isn't following me?”

Kira held her hand between the Sun and the ground.

“No. You're getting in the light's way.”

Strange stepped directly behind Boy.

“And if Max is in the light's way…”

He disappeared into Boy's shadow.

“…I can hide from the Sun.”

Max moved aside.

Sunlight fell on Strange again.

Strange frowned.

“Please stand still. I'm conducting an experiment.”

Professor smiled.

Max stretched both arms wide.

His shadow stretched across the grass.

Kira looked at it.

“The light comes from there,” she said, pointing toward the Sun. “And it keeps going until something gets in its way.”

Boy looked at his enormous shadow.

“So I'm a light stopper.”

“A temporary light stopper,” said Professor Ada.

Strange stepped behind him again.

“A very useful one.”

Boy took one step sideways.

Strange was back in the sunlight.

“Hey!”

And Max ran.

His shadow ran with him.

Strange ran after both of them.

“Come back! I need my light stopper!”
