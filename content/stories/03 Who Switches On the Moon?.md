Story 2 — Who Switches On the Moon?

Strange was standing in the garden with a torch.

It was daytime.

“What are you doing?” Kira asked.

“Sending a message.”

“To whom?”

Strange pointed the torch at the Moon.

It was pale, but clearly visible above the roof.

Max came running over.

“What does it say?”

Strange flashed the torch three times.

“Hello.”

Kira looked at the torch.

Then at the Moon.

“I don't think it can see that.”

Strange flashed twice more.

“That says Can you see this?”

Professor Ada came out carrying a basket of washing.

“Any reply?”

“Not yet.”

Max looked up.

“Why is the Moon even on?”

Kira turned to him.

“What do you mean?”

“Well…” Max pointed. “It's daytime.”

He thought for a moment.

“Who switches it on at night?”

Strange lowered the torch.

This was clearly a much more important problem.

They went inside and closed the curtains.

Professor Ada put a white stone on the table.

“Moon,” said Strange.

Max switched off the light.

The stone disappeared.

“Broken Moon,” he said.

Professor Ada turned on a desk lamp.

The stone appeared again.

Strange leaned closer.

“Fixed.”

Kira picked up the stone.

“Wait.”

She moved it away from the lamp.

Dim.

Closer.

Bright.

Then she held a book between the lamp and the stone.

Dark.

She removed the book.

Bright.

Max picked up the stone and inspected it.

“Where's the light?”

“On the stone,” said Strange.

“No. I mean inside.”

He turned it over.

Kira laughed.

“There isn't any.”

Max looked at the lamp.

Then the stone.

Then the lamp again.

“Oh.”

He put his hand in front of the lamp.

The stone went dark.

He removed it.

Bright again.

“The lamp is doing it.”

Kira looked through the window at the pale Moon.

“And what's the lamp for that?”

All four of them looked outside.

Max found it first.

“The Sun.”

For a moment nobody said anything.

Strange slowly raised his torch again.

“So the Moon doesn't have a lamp.”

“No,” said Kira.

“It borrows one.”

Strange looked impressed.

“From very far away.”

Then he pointed his torch at the Moon.

Flash. Flash. Flash.

Max groaned.

“What are you saying now?”

“Thank you.”
