A curiosity encyclopaedia for children

I Wonder

A book organised by what a child can already understand, not by subject. Every article starts from something visible in the world and a question a child actually asks — and it can only be told once the concepts underneath it are in place. Those dependencies are the whole architecture, so they are kept as data and checked on every change.

The anatomy of an article

Five beats, always in this order. The concept is never announced — it is what the child is left holding at the end.

  1. The conceptThe idea the article is really about. It is a planning label. It does not appear in the book.
  2. The phenomenonSomething the child can see for themselves, without equipment or permission.
  3. The questionHow the child would actually put it. This is the title of the article, and the only part they meet first.
  4. The experimentA thing they do with ordinary objects that makes the honest answer unavoidable.
  5. The next questionsThe doors it opens. Most of them lead out of the topic entirely — that is the point.
